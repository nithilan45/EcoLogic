## Infrastructure fixes the run required

Four changes were needed to make 48,276 calls actually converge. All affect only
*how* calls are issued — never what is asked, sampled, or graded.

- **A hard per-task timeout.** `api.py` retries 5 times at a 300 s request
  timeout, so one silently stalled socket could pin a worker for ~25 minutes.
  With 80 workers the run flatlined at **0 calls/min with load average 0.00**
  while appearing alive. Tier 1's p90 latency is ~76 s, and a full
  16,384-token generation needs ~190 s, so the ceiling is set at 300 s: high
  enough never to abort a healthy call, low enough to bound a stall.
- **Phase-granular HTTP timeouts** (connect 30 s, read 300 s, write 60 s, pool
  60 s) instead of a blanket 300 s on every phase, so a stuck connect or pool
  acquisition fails fast while long generations keep their full read budget.
- **15-second keepalive expiry and a 25-minute cap per pass.** Throughput
  started near 200 calls/min on a fresh connection pool and decayed toward zero
  as sockets went stale, so each pass now rebuilds the pool.
- **Proportional interleaving of the TRAIN and CALIBRATION queues.** The
  original TRAIN-file-first order is why the first interruption left 1,039
  TRAIN items and **zero** CALIBRATION items, which made the pre-registered
  contingency unusable. Any prefix is now ~70/30.

## Label noise the k=3 majority vote removed

Sampling at temperature 0.7 found real per-item disagreement, so the majority
vote is doing work rather than averaging identical repeats:

| Tier | Items with a 2–1 sample split | Share of 5,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 (`Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B`) | 290 | **5.8%** |
| Tier 2 (`openai/gpt-oss-20b`) | 402 | **8.0%** |
| Tier 3 (`gpt-4o`) | 201 | **4.0%** |

192 items (3.8%) had no tier whose majority grade was correct; per the
pre-registration these are labelled with the lowest-energy tier and flagged as
label noise rather than treated as ground truth. Oracle label mix: Tier 2 =
3,428, Tier 1 = 1,497, Tier 3 = 75.

So roughly 6–8% of Stage 1's single-sample per-tier labels for the two cheap
tiers were near coin flips, and Stage 7 removes that.

## Did the scaled, cleaned data improve the model itself?

This is the mechanism question behind the verdict, and the answer is mixed in a
specific way: **cross-validation improved substantially, held-out discrimination
barely moved.**

| | Stage 2 (1,200 items, single-sample labels) | Stage 7 (5,000 items, k=3 majority labels) |
|---|---|---|
| TRAIN-CV mean AUC, R1 | 0.6550 | **0.7154** |
| TRAIN-CV mean AUC, R2 | 0.6746 | **0.7162** |
| CALIBRATION AUC, Tier 1 head (R2) | 0.6558 | **0.6650** |
| CALIBRATION AUC, Tier 2 head (R2) | 0.7010 | **0.7015** |

The TRAIN-CV gain of ~0.04 is largely the labels becoming easier to predict once
denoised; on held-out data the heads moved **+0.009 and +0.0005**. Both remain
far below the ~0.85 a router would need to reliably beat a static policy already
correct ~92% of the time. This is why the test-set outcome came out as *partial
support* rather than a clean positive: the routing decisions improved enough to
narrow the accuracy gap by 1.37 pp, but the underlying probability estimates did
not get materially better at ranking items.

Two caveats on that comparison, both real: the Stage 2 and Stage 7 CALIBRATION
splits are **different item sets** from different pools, and their base rates
differ (Tier 1 0.856 vs 0.921), which alone shifts AUC. Read the flatness as "no
visible improvement", not as a precise zero.

R2 (MiniLM embeddings) won TRAIN-CV again, 0.7162 vs 0.7154 — a 0.0008 margin,
so the richer local representation is not meaningfully better than TF-IDF here.
Full grids in `s7_model_comparison.md`.

## The calibration gap barely moved

Recomputed on the new 1,500-item CALIBRATION split by the same code path:

| Quantity | Stage 6 (n=360) | Stage 7 (n=1,500) |
|---|---|---|
| Router accuracy | — | 0.9060 |
| LP-relaxed frontier at the router's energy | — | 0.9560 |
| **Calibration gap** | **+5.83 pp** | **+5.00 pp** |
| **Discreteness gap** | +0.00 pp | **+0.00 pp** |

0.83 pp closed for a 4.2× increase in data plus a label-noise fix. The
discreteness gap stayed at zero — the LP relaxation and the integer MCKP agree to
within 0.04 pp at every budget — so the headroom is still not blocked by having
to pick one tier per item. Most of the gap to the frontier remains, and it looks
structural: a limit of the feature representation, or of how predictable
per-item tier success is from the prompt at all. This design deliberately does
not distinguish those two.

## CALIBRATION-split policy comparison

Reported separately from the test-set table because it is a different split and
**not** the pre-registered comparison. No policy, variant or threshold was
selected using it: the variant was fixed by TRAIN-only CV, and τ = 0.2653 by the
pre-registered ≤10%-of-frontier budget rule (14 of 50 grid thresholds qualified;
the rule applied as written, no fallback).

n = 1,500, energy at the paper's J/1k-token rates.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Always Tier 2 | 0.9013 | [0.8852, 0.9154] | 1,394.9 | 5.9% | 0/1500/0 |
| Always Tier 3 (frontier) | 0.9060 | [0.8902, 0.9197] | 23,613.6 | 100.0% | 0/0/1500 |

| **Learned router (Stage 7)** | **0.9060** | [0.8902, 0.9197] | **2,326.0** | **9.9%** | 24/1423/53 |
| EcoLogic keyword router | 0.9113 | — | 2,757.2 | 11.7% | 1087/354/59 |

McNemar exact, learned router vs each row:

| Comparison | Δ accuracy | b / c | p | Δ energy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vs Always Tier 1 | **−1.53 pp** | 34 / 57 | **0.021** | −4.8% |
| vs Always Tier 2 | +0.47 pp | 13 / 6 | 0.167 | **+66.8%** |
| vs Always Tier 3 | +0.00 pp | 57 / 57 | 1.00 | −90.1% |
| vs Random | −0.67 pp | 35 / 45 | 0.314 | −74.9% |
| vs Oracle | −5.00 pp | 0 / 75 | 5.3e−23 | +53.5% |

**This split is less favourable to the router than the test set is**, and the
discrepancy is worth stating rather than hiding: here the router is
*significantly worse* than Always-Tier-1 (−1.53 pp, p = 0.021) while saving only
4.8% of energy, whereas on the frozen test set it finished 1.10 pp *above*
Always-Tier-2. Two honest readings, and the data does not separate them: the
1,500-item calibration split has ~4× the items and so more power to detect a
deficit, or the two splits simply differ in composition. Either way, the
test-set result is the pre-registered one and the calibration result is context
for how thin the margin is.

One structural note. On this pool Tier 1 is *more accurate* than Tier 2 (0.9213
vs 0.9013) but far more energy-hungry (2,444 J vs 1,395 J), because Tier 1 emits
~3,100 output tokens per call against Tier 2's ~427. So the cost ordering the
routing rule derives from TRAIN means is Tier 2 → Tier 1 → Tier 3, and the
router sends 1,423 of 1,500 calibration items to Tier 2. It is, in effect, an
expensive approximation of Always-Tier-2 plus a few escalations.

## Per-sample accuracy on the pool

All k=3 samples pooled at temperature 0.7, so **not** comparable to the
temperature-0 headline numbers elsewhere.

| Tier | GSM8K | MBPP | MMLU |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | 96.2% (6625/6885) | 91.7% (1128/1230) | 87.6% (6032/6885) |
| Tier 2 | 95.1% (6546/6885) | 95.4% (1173/1230) | 84.4% (5810/6885) |
| Tier 3 | 95.9% (6603/6885) | 85.3% (1049/1230) | 86.1% (5929/6885) |

Tier 3 (`gpt-4o`) scoring **lowest of the three on MBPP** (85.3%) is the clearest
anomaly here, and it reproduced at full pool size and again on the frozen test
set (86.0%), so it is not a small-sample artifact. It is reported, not explained:
the plausible causes (prompt-wrapper interaction, verbosity breaking answer
extraction, genuine weakness on this MBPP formatting) are not separated, and it
directly undercuts the "Tier 3 is the quality ceiling" assumption that the
always-frontier baseline encodes — visible in the test-set table, where
always-frontier (89.8%) trails Always-Tier-2 (92.6%).

## Resumption fidelity, for the record

Generations span roughly a 30-hour window across two credit interruptions: Tier
3 pool generations predate the Together outage, most Tier 1 and 2 generations
postdate it, and the test set's Tier 3 column was generated a day later. The
provider weights behind `gpt-4o`, `Qwen/Qwen3.5-9B` and `openai/gpt-oss-20b`
carry no immutable revision (`reproducibility_manifest.md` §3), so this run mixes
generations from different points in time. For between-tier comparisons on the
same item that is a real, probably small, and genuinely unquantified confound.
